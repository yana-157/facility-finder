From source records to searchable branches

The project began with 10,088 India facility records from the Virtue Foundation dataset in Databricks. The team reconciled facility names, branch identities, coordinates, contacts and capacity against a dated source release. Conflicting branches and locations went through a separate review. The public search includes 9,076 records with eligible coordinates. PIN, city and district searches start from the average location of matching facility records. A state can disambiguate a place name. Coordinates give the search a precise starting point. Snapshot preparation

For each of 18 services, the classifier looked for branch-specific reviewed claims, listings or mentions with supporting text. An eligible branch–service pair carries the matched term, an excerpt, the kind of evidence and source links where available. The release contains 24,651 assessments before the search applies its confidence and Fit requirements. These include 15,774 facility-record listings and 8,872 text mentions. Five others cite corroboration, a hospital source or a reviewed directory. The score gives up to 80 points for the service evidence and 20 for branch identity. A mention in a description can leave important details, such as emergency cover, unresolved. Service evidence rules

The public release was published at 06:32 UTC on 22 November. The app shows that date separately from the time TravelTime calculated a journey. An average weekday-morning journey can change as the routing provider updates its networks. Facility hours, staffing and appointment availability require confirmation with the branch.

What we tested and what changed

Can we fill missing bed counts?

The team compared source-selection rules for conflicting counts and tested a boosted-tree model on facilities with known counts. The selected rule favors the newest eligible whole-facility count from the same branch. The model had a mean absolute error of 110.22 beds across 455 held-out records. Across the full 10,088-row release, the process retained 2,713 published counts, estimated 6,833 and left 542 blank. The public cards distinguish reported beds, model estimates and missing values, and show the published estimate interval when available. Bed totals describe facility scale. Capacity experiment and release

Does the service search retrieve a documented branch?

An earlier offline benchmark exercised 16 urban and rural searches. Five produced ranked lists, seven requested an unsupported service or travel mode, and four place names could not be resolved. One of the five scored lists contained a known reference branch in its top five. Other results had incomplete reference judgments. One emergency C-section search retrieved excerpts about C-sections that did not establish emergency availability. The public interface puts the excerpt and source within reach of each recommendation and offers travel mode as an explicit choice. Benchmark queries and reference design

How much extra travel should stronger evidence justify?

The team compared confidence penalties across 27 fixed city and service searches. With a 60-minute ceiling, moving the logistic midpoint from 75 to 55 and narrowing its width from 5 to 3.5 reduced the penalty for confidence 60 to 11.60 minute-equivalents. This changed one winner and five top-three sets. In the Delhi neonatal intensive care search, the nearer of two explicit listings regained first place. Direct matches still won 24 of the 27 searches. The experiment used straight-line distances and tested the confidence adjustment. Confidence rubric and curve

When is projected workload informative?

The team modeled monthly caseload and throughput for 181,386 facility–service combinations to explore distributing additional routine work. Only 38 branch–service pairs at 19 exact branches had historical activity in the relevant scope. The public release includes those 38 projections. A broad peer forecast provides too little evidence to change a person's facility recommendation on its own. Caseload experiment and evidence coverage

How the findings shaped the interface

The restored design groups decisions into Service, Location and Options. Browse by specialty or body area, choose a starting point, then select a travel mode. The cards and numbered map pins share the same ordering so you can compare a branch's evidence with its location and contact details. The sidebar controls result count and fit weights. Original interface decisions

Recommended and fastest
Each search automatically requests TravelTime estimates for the chosen mode through the Cloudflare Worker. It checks trips of up to three hours on a typical weekday morning by driving, walking, cycling or public transport. Fastest orders eligible branches by journey time for the selected mode. A branch without a returned journey drops out of a successful route search. If the routing service fails, the page identifies the fallback and shows straight-line kilometres. A successful transit search with zero routes shows that outcome and offers a separate driving search. TravelTime matrix · Coverage by country
How Recommended orders results
Recommended groups branches by their Fit score out of 100. It shows Fit 60 or above first, then 55 to under 60, then 50 to under 55, then positive scores below 50. Within each group, the lowest journey time plus the branch’s service-confidence penalty wins. The penalty follows the earlier normalized logistic curve, with midpoint 55, width 3.5 and a range of 0 to 60 minute-equivalents. On straight-line fallback its range is 0 to 30 kilometre-equivalents. At confidence 50, 55 and 60 the penalties are about 48.4, 30 and 11.6 minutes. The Fit boundaries are a new design choice inspired by the steep part of that curve. The 27-search experiment tested confidence alone and did not validate Fit thresholds.
Fit and personal priorities
Fit starts with weights of 40% for equipment and staff, 25% for round-the-clock availability, 20% for accreditation and rating, and 15% for freshness. The sliders let you change those weights. Fit multiplies the weighted mean of documented components by the service match (100 for a direct match, 40 for a passing mention) and rounds to one decimal before sorting. Missing components drop out of the average. Service match therefore enters the recommendation through Fit once. Under Advanced options, choose and order criteria, then select Ranked criteria. The first criterion carries the most weight. The score breakdown shows how much evidence contributed. Ranking calculations
Which branches qualify
Every sort requires service confidence of at least 25 out of 100 and excludes a calculated Fit of zero. A passing service mention with 15 evidence points and 10 branch-identity points meets the confidence floor. With no documented, positively weighted Fit component, Fit is unknown. Recommended requires a positive Fit. An explicit alternative sort, such as Fastest, can include a branch with unknown Fit so the user can inspect its evidence. Across the full November 22 snapshot, 11,912 of the 24,651 branch–service assessments meet the confidence floor and have positive Fit under the default weights, including 3,513 passing mentions. A routed search narrows that set to the requested service and reachable branches.
Projected load and equity
Ease pressure on busy facilities is off by default. For a routine, non-time-critical service, it compares the first Recommended branch with alternatives in the same Fit tier and with the same kind of direct service evidence, activity units, scope and reporting period. A routed alternative must add at most 15 minutes and at most 10 straight-line kilometres. The increase in journey time plus confidence penalty must also fit within 15 minutes. On straight-line fallback both the distance increase and the confidence-adjusted increase must fit within 10 km. The comparison balances that adjusted detour against modeled workload pressure. The equity control requires comparable branch-level measures of patient-reported respect and clinical outcomes. The public snapshot has no such pairs. It discloses that gap when the option is selected.

An offline dialysis comparison in Himachal Pradesh

We tested how projected workload could change a routine dialysis recommendation. The offline experiment used the November 22 snapshot, the earlier published ranker and straight-line distances from 31.833, 77.018. This ranker added separate service-match and confidence penalties to distance and predated Fit tiers. Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose Zonal Hospital in Mandi ranked first at 16.02 km. Including workload moved Regional Hospital Kullu from second to first at 16.34 km. Kullu's estimated monthly demand was 275.92 dialysis sessions against modeled throughput of 377.60. Mandi's figures were 385.33 and 424.80. In this experiment, an extra 0.32 km reduced the modeled workload pressure. The replay pins that earlier code and snapshot. How to check a published search

Choose Dialysis and City. Enter Jaipur, Rajasthan for driving, walking or cycling. For public transport, use Mumbai, Maharashtra. Choose Fastest to see the shortest eligible journey. In the 24 November routing checks, the provider returned 14 driving, 13 walking and 14 cycling routes in Jaipur and 39 transit routes in Mumbai. These are route counts before the app applies its Fit and confidence rules. Jaipur's transit search returned none, which also let us test the empty result.

To see how Fit can change the first result, choose Trauma / emergency, City, Jaipur, Rajasthan and Driving with the default weights. In a signed-out check, Recommended put Shekhawati Hospital & Research Centre first with Fit 61.5, service confidence 60 and an 18.8-minute estimate. Fastest put Unikare Hospital first at 5.4 minutes, with Fit 15.4 and service confidence 25. Switching sorts reused the same route estimates.

Before publication, the release check hashes the exact facility snapshot, requests each mode through the same Worker the browser uses, and checks the Fastest and Recommended orders against the returned seconds and the snapshot’s Fit and service-confidence scores.
